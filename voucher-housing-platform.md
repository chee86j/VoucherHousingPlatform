Every day, I meet families who have government rental assistance and landlords who have available apartments. On paper, these people should be able to find each other and complete a rental. In reality, the process is fragmented, confusing and painfully slow.

A family may have a CityFHEPS, FHEPS, Section 8 or HASA voucher, but that alone does not mean they are ready to rent. We still have to determine:

• Is the voucher active?
• Is it a new voucher or a transfer?
• How many bedrooms are approved?
• What is the maximum rent?
• What portion does the tenant pay?
• Which utilities are included?
• Is an updated breakdown letter required?
• Has the household composition changed?
• Is the caseworker responsive?
• Is the client still in a shelter, or are they renting privately?
• Are all identification, income and household documents available?

Many clients do not fully understand their own voucher. Some send screenshots instead of complete documents. Some have been told they are approved but are still waiting for the official paperwork. Others start viewing apartments before completing a required transfer or recertification.

Then there is the landlord side.

Many landlords are willing to accept vouchers, but they do not understand pre-clearance, inspections, utility calculations, HPD registration, ownership documents, leases, tax forms or the landlord package. They become nervous when several weeks pass without an update. If nobody can explain what is happening, they may move on to another tenant.

The housing specialist or caseworker is supposed to connect the pieces, but many are managing a large caseload. Emails may go unanswered. Staff members go on vacation or transfer departments. Sometimes nobody knows who has responsibility for the file. A package may be submitted with one missing document and then sit untouched until someone follows up repeatedly.

There is no single place where everyone can see:

• The tenant’s actual readiness
• The apartment information
• What documents have been received
• What is still missing
• Whether pre-clearance was submitted
• Whether the lease and landlord package were accepted
• Whether an inspection is required or has been scheduled
• Who currently has the file
• What the next action is
• How long the case has been sitting at each stage

I am currently holding much of this together manually through calls, emails, text messages, PDFs, spreadsheets and personal relationships.

I speak with the tenant, collect the application, verify the voucher, communicate with the caseworker, explain the program to the landlord, calculate the rent based on utilities, prepare documents, coordinate showings, follow up on pre-clearance, schedule inspections and continue pushing until the family receives the keys.

The most frustrating part is that the apartment often exists, the rental assistance exists, and the family genuinely needs the home. The transaction still fails because the information does not move efficiently between the people involved.

Large families are hit especially hard. There are very few three-, four- and five-bedroom apartments available to voucher holders. When one becomes available, several families may qualify, but it is difficult to determine quickly which family is truly document-ready. Every unnecessary delay risks losing the apartment.

There is also no reliable private network connecting responsible landlords, licensed brokers, qualified tenants and housing professionals. Social media generates leads, but it also creates noise. Hundreds of people may respond to one apartment without providing enough information to determine whether they qualify. Sensitive documents then get scattered across text messages, email accounts and phones.

I believe the opportunity begins with organizing this world.

We need a secure system that can build a verified client profile, match the household to appropriate inventory, track every required document and clearly identify the next responsible person. It should help us know, before scheduling a showing, whether the tenant is actually ready and whether the apartment fits the voucher.

It must also protect applicants, follow fair-housing rules and handle personal information responsibly. The goal is not to create a secret list or decide who deserves housing. The goal is to remove preventable delays, make the process transparent to authorized participants and help more families complete legitimate rentals.

I am not committed to one business model yet. It might become a private database, a workflow platform, a placement service, or a combination of technology and hands-on coordination. Before deciding that, I want us to map what actually happens from the first tenant inquiry through move-in and identify exactly where deals are breaking down.

I have direct access to the people experiencing the problem: tenants, landlords, brokers, housing specialists and shelter staff. I also have active rental files that can show us the process in real time.

I would like you to look at this world with me and see what we can build.
